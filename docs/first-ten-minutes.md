# The first ten minutes with Brickyard

A walk you can take in one sitting, at a desk or on a tablet, from the
invite link to a build of your own that you can find again.  Each step
says what you should see; where you see something else, that is worth
writing down.

**You need:** your invite link, and a recent Chrome, Edge, Firefox or
Safari.  A mouse helps; a tablet works, with the limits noted at the
end.

## Minute 0-1: the link

Open your invite link.  Brickyard opens on a small sample build, with a
**Start here** card over it.  Read the card: it is this page in four
lines.  **Got it** puts it away; **Start here** in the top bar brings
it back.

## Minutes 1-3: see one work

On the card, choose **Open the Gearbox** (or **Gearbox** in the top
bar).  You see two gray Technic bricks, a motor, two axles, a small
gear, a big gear and a wheel.  Press **Run** at the lower right.

- The motor's axle and the small gear turn; the big gear and the wheel
  turn the other way, slower.  **Stop** stops them.
- The **Build** report on the right says *1 gear pair meshes* and
  *Motor (2) drives Wheel 5x2 (7) at 1/3 speed, reversed*: eight teeth
  driving twenty-four.

## Minutes 3-6: a gear train of your own

Choose **Gear frame** in the top bar.  You see two long Technic bricks
holding four axles, two studs apart, and a motor turning the first
axle.  There are no gears yet: they are yours to choose.

1. Click (or tap) the first axle, the one in the motor; it glows.  Click
   **Gear 8** in the parts bin (under *Technic*): it goes onto that axle,
   between the bricks.
2. Click the next axle, then **Gear 24**.
3. Do the same for the last two axles: **Gear 8**, then **Gear 24**.
4. Press **Run**: the train turns, each gear the other way from its
   neighbor.  The **Build** report lists which gears mesh with which.

Neighbors mesh when their teeth add up to 32: 8 and 24, or 16 and 16.
Two 24s on neighboring axles do not fit side by side, so the second goes
onto the end of its axle, outside the bricks, and meshes with nothing;
**Undo** takes it away again.  Every part you click joins the part that
glows.  When gears hide an axle's middle, click the end of it that
sticks out past the bricks.

Starting from nothing instead: **Empty build**, then **Motor** (under
*Robot*), **Axle 4** and **Gear 8**, each joining the last, and **Run**.

## Minutes 6-8: change it

- Click (or tap) a part to select it.  Drag it to slide it; it snaps
  into place when you let go, and what it carries comes along.
- Buttons appear at the right of the build while a part is selected:
  **Turn**, **Tilt** and **Roll** a quarter turn, **Swing** 15 degrees
  about the pin or axle it hangs on, **Copy**, **Delete**, **Let go**.
  With a keyboard, **R**, **T**, **E**, **[** **]**, **C**, **Del** and
  **Esc** do the same.
- **Undo** and **Redo** at the lower left (or Ctrl+Z and Ctrl+Y) take
  back any change, one at a time.  Try deleting the motor, then Undo.

## Minutes 8-9: on the table

Choose **Robot build** in the top bar, then **Table**.  The robot stands
on a competition-style table with a program already written in the
panel.  Press **Run program**: the robot drives, pushes the balls, and
the log says where it ended and what it scored.  **Pybricks** writes
the same program out for a real SPIKE Prime hub.

## Minute 10: come back to it

Close the tab.  Open your invite link again: you are back on the build
you last had open, as you left it.  Builds save themselves as you go;
nothing needs saving by hand.  **Your builds**, in the top bar, lists
the others you have opened in this browser.

## What to write down

Anything that made you stop and wonder: a part that went somewhere you
did not expect, a word you did not understand, a step on this page that
did not happen as described.  Those are what a student meets too.

## Known limits, so they do not surprise you

- A frame of your own, axles held the right distance apart, is built by
  dragging axles into a Technic brick's holes; clicking alone puts an
  axle in a hole you do not choose.  The Gear frame is that frame, ready.
- On a tablet, tap to select and use the buttons beside the build in
  place of the keys.  Dragging a part by touch should work but has not
  yet been tried on a real tablet: say how it goes.  Nudging a part one
  stud at a time (the arrow keys) and moving a part alone without what
  it carries (Shift) still need a keyboard.
- The box at the top ("Describe what to build") asks a language model
  to build for you.  Each prompt draws on Brickyard's shared allowance,
  so use it, but not in a loop.
- **Your builds** lives in the browser: another browser or device does
  not see the list, though every build's own address opens it anywhere.
